ParticleGAN / a single-transition experiment

Learning a transition,
without learning a trajectory.

Three generators learn where a particle is, how it moves, and where it ends up. An encoder connects the first two outputs back to the generator for the next state.

This is a two-route toy around a circular obstacle. Action means displacement. Each training record contains just (st, at, st+1). The images below show independent transitions, not simulated rollouts.

What is working?

The shared-state encoder improves the fixed benchmark’s joint distribution score by 14.6% over the earlier adversarial-only leader. Both encoder versions improve held-out transition consistency and coverage.

What is still missing?

This is a working distribution-modeling experiment, not yet a general world model. Try scene 4 in the held-out split to see the extrapolation weakness.

Here the exact rule is simply st+1 = st + at. The networks do not enforce it. Prediction errors remain substantial relative to a typical step, and small action changes outside the route distribution produce poor responses.

These geometries have been reused for model selection, so this is a development benchmark. Every plot uses saved samples from completed checkpoints. No trajectories are reconstructed by joining points across times, and no good-looking samples were selected individually.

A description you can share

We tried learning a distribution of individual state/action/next-state triples with three generators sharing a 1,024-component MoG prior. An encoder maps (state, action) back into that latent space, giving a synthetic G1/G2 → E → G3 path. Sharing a time-conditioned state discriminator improves joint SW1 by 14.6% on our fixed route toy benchmark. Coverage and off-distribution action response remain weak; this adds paired supervision and is not yet a general world model.
