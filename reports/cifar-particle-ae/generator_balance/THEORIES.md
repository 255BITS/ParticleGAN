# Working hypotheses

1. **Relative G update size.** D separates fixed real/fake distributions well, but this does not persist in the game. Halving only G LR tests whether smaller responses permit more useful feedback at unchanged D cost. Success requires sustained FID improvement, not just higher AUC. Failure at this one ratio would not eliminate all update schedules.
2. **Feedback directions do not reward the remaining image errors.** Strong D-only separation and larger gradient norms did not predict better joint FID. Better classification is insufficient to establish useful gradients. If the LR scout fails, a discriminator robustness/intervention test remains justified. Pretrained representation capacity itself was not isolated by the earlier ResNet34 swap because trained heads inherited different feature coordinates.
3. **Restricted image variation around particles.** The latent table has effective covariance rank ~64/64 at both 10k and 50k, so global latent collapse is not supported. This does not establish image coverage. A read-only probe will compare original sampling with zero/doubled local noise and decompose within/between particle image-feature variance. An improvement from changing inference noise is a sampler sensitivity result, not proof that increasing training noise will help.
4. **Reconstruction conflict.** It can occur locally, but E-only training still plateaus. Direct reconstruction gradients on G/prior are not necessary for the plateau. Removing them has not established a fix.
5. **G capacity.** Extra CNN layers and a much larger transformer did not resolve FID under the shared recipe. This weakens a simple parameter-count explanation; optimization of those architectures and their interaction with D remain confounds.
6. **Measurement.** Original FID protocol was reproduced within 0.00003. Current tests retain the same cached real reference, 50k generated samples and EMA model. Do not compare diagnostic reconstruction FID or reduced-sample smoke FID with the unconditional benchmark.
