# EMA-centred R1: the family between R1 and EMA-centring

**Verdict: no β > 0 improves on gs2's R1, so nothing goes to the full suite.** Within-mode covariance does not improve
anywhere in the family. The only arm that reaches gs2's worst cov ratio while keeping the rings nearly clean is β = .2,
which is effectively R1: .146 vs .142, 2 ring fails, img_bars4 11/24 vs 19/24. Every step toward the EMA slope makes
the covariance and the rings worse, or trades one for the other.

## What was run

The real-side term replaces R1. The fake cap and everything else in gs2_c03_lr2_d05 stay the same:

    pen = c/2 * [ w * mean ||grad D(r) - beta * grad Dbar(r)||^2 / d  +  mean relu(||grad D(f)||/sqrt d - kappa)^2 ]

- Dbar is K3P's parameter-EMA critic. It is the existing `CriticAnchor`/`RobustCriticAnchor` on the GANTrainer route and
  the suite's `SwapAnchor` on the custom-loop route. `reg_anchor_decay` sets its decay and `reg_anchor_weight` = w sets
  the weight. It is forced on from the first penalty call at constant LR.
- The spike guard reads `exp_avg_sq`, as in gs2. So β = 0 is gs2 exactly. β = 1, decay .999, w 1 is `nr_none_anchor`
  except for the guard buffer, which is why it was rerun as `e1_b1_d0.999_w1`. The guard buffer turns out not to
  matter: 34 vs 38 ring fails, worst cov .012 vs .013.
- Code: `settle="emar1"` in `nr_adapter.py` (`ema_centred_r1`, `e1_anchor_fn`, `emar1_real`). Arms are generated by
  `gen_arms.py` into `arms_e1.json`. Launch with `e1_screen.sh` and `e1_followup.sh`, and build the table with
  `summarize_e1.py`.
- Tests (`test_nr_terms.py`, 91 pass with `tests/test_oadam_amsgrad.py`):
  - At β = 0, the penalty value and all parameter gradients are bit-identical (`torch.equal`) to the stock s == 1 R1
    penalty.
  - At β = 1, the term matches `anchor_prox` (rtol 1e-12) for w 1 and w 3.
  - Linear-critic known values hold.
  - The receipt records β, decay, weight and anchor_started.
- End to end, `e1_b0_d0.999_w1` reproduces gs2's `metric` and `detail` on all 7 screen tasks.
- Receipts: all 77 cells pass `receipt_ok`. Each `nr_receipt.json` has an `emar1` block holding β, the decay and weight
  read from the live anchor and kernel objects, anchor_started, the number of EMA evals, and the max and mean real RMS
  slope. Every decay matched its arm, including the custom-loop SwapAnchor. R1 evals and stock penalty calls were 0.
- 11 arms x 7 screen tasks ran with 4 jobs per launcher on a shared machine (load 90-170 from other sessions), in about
  45 min of wall time. All runs exited with rc=0.

## How β and decay trade ring stability against within-mode covariance

**β, the pull toward zero slope.** At the RpGAN/R1 fixed point, D is flat on the data. So on reals, R1 is not
penalizing a correct slope: the correct slope there is 0. Centring on β·ḡ removes a fraction of that pull. Without it,
the real-side slope grows:

- Mean real RMS slope on grid100: .026 at β 0, .052 at β .5, .12-.17 at β .8-1.
- Max real RMS slope on the natives: 4.9 at β 0, 12.7-18.4 at β ≥ .5.

A critic that keeps a slope inside a mode pushes the particles along it, toward its ridge, which thins the mode on one
axis. So the worst eigenvalue ratio falls from .14 to .00-.02. The damage is not monotone in β, but every β ≥ .5 at
decay .999 is worse than R1. β .2 is too small to change anything measurable.

**Decay, what "centred" means.** A slow EMA (.999, about 1000 steps) centres on an old slope. That slope is itself
steep, so the term acts as a prox that holds D near its past self. The rings get partial stability from this (34-38
fails vs 364 with nothing), but the covariance collapses, as above.

A fast EMA (.9) makes ḡ ≈ g. At β 1 the term becomes a penalty on the slope's step-to-step change only, which is close
to no real-side term at all:
- Covariance returns to the R1-free level: .140, the same as `nr_none_none`'s .15.
- The ring loses all settling: 323 fails, and ring real RMS slope 1.06 against .13 for R1.
- Decay .99 is worse on both counts (487 fails, cov .022).

**Decay .9 with β < 1.** Here the term is ≈ (1-β)²·R1. So `e1_b0.5_d0.9` is a quarter-strength R1. It lands between the
two: cov .080, 117 ring fails.

**Weight.** At β 1, raising the weight only strengthens the prox. At w 3 and w 10 the rings get worse (221 and 391
fails) and the native HQ drops (rotated100 falls to .855 at w 3 and .585 at w 10). two_pole improves (18 and 19 of 24),
because it is the one task that likes a stiff critic.

The trade-off is one-dimensional. Ring stability comes only from pulling the real slope toward zero, which is R1's
job. Anything that weakens that pull (β > 0 with a slow EMA, or an effective (1-β)² < 1 with a fast one) costs the
rings, and none of it frees the within-mode covariance.

## Recommendations

1. **Keep zero-centred R1 in k3p_simple.** The premise that R1 penalizes correct slope does not hold on reals at the
   fixed point. The measured effect of removing the zero-pull is steeper real-side slopes and thinner modes, not wider
   ones.
2. **Stop searching on the real-side regularizer for the covariance gate.** There have now been 20 no-R1 arms and 11
   EMA-centred arms, and none moves the worst cov ratio above gs2's .14-.15. k3p_stock reaches .57 with R1 present.
   The difference lies in what stock has and simple lacks: instance noise, the annealed LR with the anchor
   blend, and the direct-particle response. Ablate those one at a time on gs2 with R1 kept.
3. If a softer R1 is still wanted, β .2 at decay .999 is harmless on native (3 of 3 HQ ≈ gs2, cov .146), but it costs
   img_bars4 (11 vs 19) and gives 2 multishift fails. There is no reason to adopt it.
4. Do not run β at decay .9 or .99, or the higher weights, further. Each is dominated by gs2 on both axes.
