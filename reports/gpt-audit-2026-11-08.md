# ParticleGAN scientific and engineering audit — 2026-11-08

**Assessment: useful research software with substantial reproducibility infrastructure, but several current benchmark paths do not measure the scientific questions their reports claim. Repair those paths before spending on more formulation search or using the inventory to establish superiority.** The public trainer looks considerably stronger than some of the surrounding experiment and publication code.

Reviewed `develop` at `528807d4b26db979c8e77d0543d266514c8e14d9`, package version 0.9.7. This is a source and evidence audit, supported by CPU software checks and zero-update diagnostic probes. No new scientific training campaign, seed experiment, GPU work, qualification change, or historical regrading was performed. Findings concern the inspected implementation; they do not retroactively establish how every archived run executed.

The [technique inventory](forge/technique-inventory.md) remains the single current leaderboard. This report supplies interpretation and a repair order, rather than another ranking.

### A1. The neural-imputer test evaluates a different sampler

The [task declaration](../configs/forge/tasks/misgan_block_neural_imputer.json) explicitly asks for `G_i([x*m, m, z_i])` on ambiguous held-out queries **without ppost**. The [problem page](forge/problems.md#misgan_block_neural_imputer) says all eight selected families pass that question.

However, [`MisGANNeuralImputerFixture.observe`](https://github.com/255BITS/ParticleGAN/blob/528807d4b26db979c8e77d0543d266514c8e14d9/benchmarks/toy_audit/api_conditionals.py#L2189) checks `_subspace_ready`. When true, its `predict_fn` generates a bank with `ema_G_x`, calls `misgan_ppost_indices(..., sigma=0.005)`, and returns posterior-resampled completions. It never calls `G_i` in that branch. The training helper sets this flag after its completion procedure becomes active.

I constructed the public fixture without training, set the flag to exercise that exact branch, replaced the neural-imputer forward with a function that would raise, and instrumented ppost. A two-query observation returned draws with shape `[2, 2, 8]`: **one ppost call, zero neural-imputer calls**. The scorer was replaced only to invoke the prediction closure on a small panel; this was a routing check, not a quality measurement.

**Implication:** passing this path does not demonstrate successful neural imputation without posterior resampling. Preserve the affected receipts and their original identities, but attach a scientific scope correction. Restore a separate evaluator that actually invokes `G_i`; retain posterior resampling as its own declared task. A meaningful regression should fail if the no-ppost evaluator calls ppost, and should demonstrate that damaging `G_i` changes its measured result.

### A2. Benchmark hosts contain additional training algorithms

Two particularly consequential examples:

- [`_apply_scaled_res16_dispersion_step`](https://github.com/255BITS/ParticleGAN/blob/528807d4b26db979c8e77d0543d266514c8e14d9/benchmarks/toy_audit/api_vectors.py#L248) estimates up to 100 cluster centres from real batches, estimates within-cluster spread, resets the generator to its paired identity, and moves prior rows onto a constructed cluster-and-offset arrangement. Late in the run, the interpolation rate becomes **1.0**. It also copies the resulting locations into EMA/averaged tables. Both [`VectorFixture.step`](https://github.com/255BITS/ParticleGAN/blob/528807d4b26db979c8e77d0543d266514c8e14d9/benchmarks/toy_audit/api_vectors.py#L1224) and the [formulaic deep-grid runner](https://github.com/255BITS/ParticleGAN/blob/528807d4b26db979c8e77d0543d266514c8e14d9/ops/formulaic_coverage.py#L364) invoke it after `trainer.step`.
- [`_apply_observed_subspace_step`](https://github.com/255BITS/ParticleGAN/blob/528807d4b26db979c8e77d0543d266514c8e14d9/benchmarks/toy_audit/api_conditionals.py#L1369) performs masked alternating least squares, kernel completion, density-peak extraction and particle placement. Beginning halfway through the declared horizon, it zeros and reconstructs `G_x` and `G_m` weights and overwrites prior tables, followed by EMA replacement. Both MisGAN training loops invoke it after optimizer updates. The code includes task-specific choices such as 100 peaks, a 256-particle allocation and target in-plane sigma `0.019`.

These algorithms use training observations; the inspected code does **not** justify an accusation of held-out-label leakage. The problem is attribution and ownership. A common auxiliary solver can make every family pass without establishing that their adversarial update rules learned the solution. Direct prior writes also need to obey the recipe's learned/frozen policy.

**Recommendation:** describe existing measurements as assisted-host results wherever applicable. Move reusable update mechanisms into explicitly declared recipe capabilities or give the assisted task a separate identity. Keep an unassisted control, and declare the solver's data access, computation and horizon dependence. Do not use the eight-family PASS on the scaled grid as evidence that ordinary deep GAN optimization has been solved.

### A3. “Unchanged selected recipe” is not an accurate contract for every coverage cell

[`adapt_candidate_for_task`](https://github.com/255BITS/ParticleGAN/blob/528807d4b26db979c8e77d0543d266514c8e14d9/ops/formulaic_coverage.py#L106) changes E22's prior mechanism to particles plus a `NormalNoiseManipulator`, disables its critic guard settings, and changes output-noise policy. It also forces input/output training noise off on DDGAN and stretch-fixture tasks. The [fixture runner](https://github.com/255BITS/ParticleGAN/blob/528807d4b26db979c8e77d0543d266514c8e14d9/ops/formulaic_coverage.py#L435) again replaces noise settings when binding the recipe. These are training-mechanism changes, beyond task-owned architecture and resource bindings. A dry adaptation of the E22 card reproduced these deltas.

Separately, [MisGAN recovery observation](https://github.com/255BITS/ParticleGAN/blob/528807d4b26db979c8e77d0543d266514c8e14d9/benchmarks/toy_audit/api_conditionals.py#L1898) samples `ema_prior_x` through `ema_G_x`; neural-imputer observation likewise selects EMA objects. Their Forge cards declare `public_prior_without_output_noise`, and the screening protocol requires live weights. During the overwrite phase some EMA and live objects are deliberately made equal, but that does not establish a general live-sampling contract. `_schedule_recipe` also substitutes a KA2 schedule for stationarity/continuous recipes in this host.

The [formulaic result payload](https://github.com/255BITS/ParticleGAN/blob/528807d4b26db979c8e77d0543d266514c8e14d9/ops/formulaic_coverage.py#L588) records family/candidate labels, outcomes, metrics and duration, but does not include the complete resolved recipe or actual observed serving law.

**Recommendation:** require the same resolved recipe and sampling witnesses used by ordinary Forge admission on every comparison path. Necessary adaptations should receive explicit candidate/task identities and deltas. Publish their results as those adaptations; do not transfer them into the original selected recipe's claimed common-contract comparison.

### A4. Some checkpoint contracts omit behavior-changing state

The MisGAN providers' [`state_dict` / `load_state_dict`](https://github.com/255BITS/ParticleGAN/blob/528807d4b26db979c8e77d0543d266514c8e14d9/benchmarks/toy_audit/api_conditionals.py#L2250) omit data, latent and noise generator states; the completion cache and `_subspace_ready`; and actual EMA generator/prior histories. Loading reconstructs EMA objects from live weights instead of restoring their saved averages.

The fresh-object probe confirmed that a saved fixture with `_subspace_ready=True` reloads with the flag false. By A1, this changes the evaluator from posterior resampling to neural inference. This is a behavioral change on reload, before any further training.

The scaled-grid helper stores its cluster buffers, centres, spread estimate and offsets in `trainer._scaled_res16_emp_state`. Neither [`VectorFixture.state_dict`](https://github.com/255BITS/ParticleGAN/blob/528807d4b26db979c8e77d0543d266514c8e14d9/benchmarks/toy_audit/api_vectors.py#L1313) nor [`GANTrainer.state_dict`](https://github.com/255BITS/ParticleGAN/blob/528807d4b26db979c8e77d0543d266514c8e14d9/particlegan/training.py#L654) includes that helper state. A restart can therefore discard the history that determines later particle placement. This latter continuation consequence is inferred from the missing state; I did not run a full-budget restart comparison.

**Recommendation:** version complete host checkpoints and validate them before mutation. Test continuation in a newly constructed fixture, including immediately before and after auxiliary mechanisms activate. Compare next data/latent draws, predictions, serving selection and full state. Same-object terminal reloads cannot detect absent fields that remain attached to the old object. Keep older receipts pinned to their original contracts.

### A5. Import order changes the architecture under test

[`api_vectors.py`](https://github.com/255BITS/ParticleGAN/blob/528807d4b26db979c8e77d0543d266514c8e14d9/benchmarks/toy_audit/api_vectors.py#L437) assigns:

```python
SimpleMLPGenerator = ScaledSimpleMLPGenerator
lib.toy_models.SimpleMLPGenerator = ScaledSimpleMLPGenerator
```

The replacement enables LayerNorm by default for depth ≥16. In a fresh interpreter, constructing `lib.toy_models.SimpleMLPGenerator(2, 128, 16, 2)` before this import produced **zero LayerNorm layers**; constructing it afterwards produced **16**. Callers that captured the original class earlier retain it, so process import order selects the architecture.

The [problem description](forge/problems.md#grid100_mlp16) calls this an unnormalized 16-layer stress test. A normalization repair is a useful distinct cohort, but cannot silently become that original test. `ResidualMLPGenerator` is also aliased to the scaled implementation, which warrants a corresponding declared-architecture check.

**Recommendation:** remove process-wide benchmark monkeypatches. Use explicit factories and separately named task variants. Bind the actual module tree and parameter/buffer shapes to the task's architecture witness. Test construction in separate interpreters with both import orders.

### A6. Binary publication loses information needed to diagnose failures

The [inventory](forge/technique-inventory.md#reading-the-results) explicitly counts constructor refusals, dependency blocks and other non-passing outcomes as binary FAIL. The [publication code](https://github.com/255BITS/ParticleGAN/blob/528807d4b26db979c8e77d0543d266514c8e14d9/reports/forge/regenerate_technique_inventory.py#L3453) implements that display policy. Elsewhere, the [problem list](forge/problems.md) and glossary distinguish these from numerical failures.

The underlying [attempt ledger](forge/attempt-coverage.json) still has 174 BLOCKED, 9 ERROR, 3 INCOMPLETE and 19 TIMEOUT records, alongside 457 FAIL and 385 PASS, across 1,047 attempted recipe/test pairs. Those are different causes and scopes, not a pooled performance estimate. “Attempted every cell” does not mean “measured every numerical gate.”

The selected table's **115 view requirements represent only 36 distinct tests**. Repeating a test across views is legitimate policy accounting, but supplies no extra scientific replication. Diagnostic projections, source-bound ordinary readouts and historical qualifications also answer different questions, even when displayed together.

**Recommendation:** retain a conservative binary eligibility field, but label its negative value “not satisfied” or “not qualified.” Alongside it, display the original scientific/operational status and denominator of actual numerical measurements. Lead comparisons with distinct tasks, compatible cohorts and attained qualified tier; keep repeated view totals as policy detail. Make generator templates enforce the same terminology as the glossary.

### A7. The current evidence supports research selection, not general superiority

There are good safeguards: the [frozen calibration criteria](../configs/forge/calibration/criteria-v1.json) require compatible positive/negative references, bounded false acceptance/rejection, complete costs and a sufficiently cheap screen. The [calibration protocol](forge/CURRENT_CALIBRATION_PROTOCOL.md) states that adoption remains unaccepted. Preserve that standard.

The inventory additionally reports no untuned holdout for the selected searched K3P, KA2, R1/R2 and release-0.7 recipes: selection used Tier ≤3. It reports markedly unequal development effort, including **85 registered BCAP searches / 175 grid configurations and 473 declared candidate cards**, versus 11 BISPF cards and one E22 card. Absence of a registered search does not mean a method was never developed against the benchmark.

The [earlier evidence review](forge/WHAT_WORKED_REVIEW_20261015.md) already documents ranking changes caused by task redesign, acquisition/hold splits and horizon choices. The [Modern GAN baseline readout](forge/R3GAN_BASELINE_READOUT.md) correctly limits its short direct-particle failure; it does not reproduce that paper's image convergence claim. A screen that favors fast particle movement may reject useful slower trainers.

**Recommendation:** freeze a small claim-specific confirmation set of previously unused tasks/data laws before selecting the next recipe, with equal prospective tuning budgets and one global configuration. Do not call another sample of the same tuned synthetic distribution a task-level holdout. Calibrate a feasible new profile using declared gates and budgets; do not fill an already infeasible matrix or relax it retrospectively. Under the repository rule, do not launch seed-only experiments. Any later robustness stage remains subject to the existing preregistered promotion prerequisites and explicit authorization.

### A8. Architecture, surrogate gradients and automatic gates need narrower claims

[`BISPFGates`](https://github.com/255BITS/ParticleGAN/blob/528807d4b26db979c8e77d0543d266514c8e14d9/particlegan/_bispf/gates.py) declares substantial shape- and call-count-dependent behavior: the automatic categorical boundary is width 170, continuous-flow behavior starts after 500 penalty calls, dense multimode behavior after 1,000, and a late two-dimensional thermal rule after 1,650. A single configuration can legitimately contain these rules, but this is a composite method whose transfer across dimensions, batch sizes and horizons needs evidence. Constant learning rate alone does not establish clock invariance. The [explicit input-domain API](../docs/bispf-input-domains.md) is a useful improvement over guessing semantics from width.

[`TanhRailGuard.backward`](https://github.com/255BITS/ParticleGAN/blob/528807d4b26db979c8e77d0543d266514c8e14d9/benchmarks/toy_audit/api_vectors.py#L77) clips the upstream gradient, injects a restoring term, and divides the chain-rule term by the output scale. Its backward is a surrogate update rule rather than the derivative of its forward. The zero-update float64 probe measured:

| Input `u` | Autograd derivative | Central finite difference (`h=1e-6`) | Gradient with zero upstream signal |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 0.1837293 | 0.9645787 | 0 |
| 10 | 0.3960656 | 0.0555347 | 0.3854875 |

This may be intentional stabilization, but it supplies an additional optimization mechanism. Its effect should not be attributed solely to normalization, ordinary tanh, or the compared trainer. If an exact derivative is intended, the probe demonstrates a defect; if a surrogate is intended, name and version it accordingly and test the intended update law.

## What is already good

- **The public core has meaningful engineering contracts.** `GANTrainer` separates sampling from training streams, validates checkpoint compatibility, saves independent state, and rejects several incompatible configurations. The public deterministic initializer and named streams provide a useful foundation for matched comparisons.
- **Forge preserves unusually detailed negative evidence.** Source/runtime/task identities, immutable declarations, explicit budgets, continuation parents, concluded readouts and archive receipts are valuable. The remaining issues are enforcement gaps in particular execution paths, rather than a reason to discard the framework.
- **Metrics are substantially better than judging animations.** Mode mass, precision, covariance, distribution shape, sustained terminal checks, paired reconstruction and frozen adaptation controls ask distinct questions. Oracle samplers and constructive witnesses distinguish metric feasibility and model capacity from successful training. Keep GIFs as illustrations of actual observed training.
- **The reporting is often candid.** The README calls BISPF a research default; its displayed 100-Gaussian failure is labelled FAIL. The prior study preserves negative results and does not claim a resolved image-scale effect. The optimization readout distinguishes a faster polynomial update from exact SVD equivalence.
- **Release engineering has real checks.** CI builds wheels, imports installed packages outside the checkout, checks base dependencies separately, verifies nonoverlapping test shards and publishes tested distributions through Trusted Publishing.

## Engineering improvements beyond the scientific blockers

**Reduce the maintenance surface around the core.** The inspected tree has 70 library Python files / 21,832 lines, 121 Forge Python files / 42,675 lines, and 512 test Python files / 113,496 lines. There are also 2,457 Python files / 620,218 lines under reports, including frozen source reproductions. These counts are scope measurements, not quality scores. In particular, the publication wrapper is 5,371 lines and tests import it from suite-wide `conftest.py`. Separate active publication logic, experiment execution and frozen replay support into explicit modules and test groups. Preserve archived bytes and identities while doing so.

**Make critical lint classes strict.** The current lint check passes against a baseline of 302 findings, including 3 F821 and 93 F811 findings. Some may be intentional fixtures or static-analysis limitations; they are not all demonstrated runtime defects. Review undefined-name/redefinition cases individually and keep any exemptions local and justified. A green baseline proves no drift, not a clean codebase. Add semantic tests for A1–A5 rather than relying on more assertion-count or publication-snapshot coverage.

**Separate supported dependency ranges from tested environments.** Metadata allows Torch `>=2.6,<3`; ordinary CI installs the current satisfying version rather than exercising that minimum. Python 3.11 runs only one deterministic quarter of the suite, while Python 3.12 covers all four shards. Add a small minimum-dependency public-API job and a complete minimum-Python job when practical. Preserve resolved environments, Torch build/BLAS details and CPU dispatch settings with reproducible scientific artifacts; the legacy toy workflow already demonstrates why hardware arithmetic matters. Keep a separately admitted CUDA software check for CUDA-specific fast paths, since ordinary CPU CI deselects those tests.

**Finish adoption metadata.** The README says MIT, but there is no root license file or project license declaration in `pyproject.toml`. Add the intended license text and package metadata, then verify the built distributions include it. This is an adoption/documentation gap, not a legal assessment.

**Regenerate explanations from the same evidence as tables.** README's explicitly dated October counts and the problem page's October passer narratives coexist with November tables. For example, the ring-extension narrative describes the selected BISPF cell as unmeasured, while its adjacent selected-result table displays PASS. Preserve dated historical analysis, but label it locally and generate current summaries from the same source-bound records. Also explain prominently that retained-publication freshness and current-source measurement freshness are separate.

The standing [develop → master PR #528](https://github.com/255BITS/ParticleGAN/pull/528) exists and includes the required link to the current develop inventory. No PR change was necessary.

## Experiment interpretation and recommended order of work

The most useful causal result is the [2026-10-25 matched prior ablation](prior-ablation-2026-10-25/README.md). Among the 15 tasks graded in all three prior arms, learned particles pass 7 versus 5 for each control. Ring16 high-quality fraction is 0.95 learned, 0.61 frozen and 0.53 fresh Gaussian. That supports a **scoped seed-0 toy precision benefit**. The decisive mode-count passes have zero slack and no recorded noise floor, so the pass-count difference needs more careful evaluation uncertainty analysis.

The same report's CIFAR FID50k values, 79.7 / 108.5 / 82.7, are single final EMA measurements. It documents much larger training/checkpoint variation and explicitly leaves the image-scale ordering unresolved. Evaluation repeats can measure conditional sampling uncertainty; checkpoint fluctuations and repeated evaluation are not independent estimates of training variability. Use the result to narrow the claim, not to promise a general image-quality advantage.

Recommended sequence:

1. **Repair measurement and attribution first.** Resolve A1–A5 with small software controls, no new full-budget search. Preserve old receipts and annotate affected claims. Exit condition: actual sampler, actual recipe, actual architecture and complete fresh-process restoration agree with their declarations.
2. **Repair the single publication.** Retain operational statuses, show distinct-task coverage and separate qualified results from diagnostic projections. Exit condition: each headline count traces to compatible evidence and readers can tell why a cell did not pass without opening raw logs.
3. **Finish a feasible calibration design and task-level confirmation split.** Query Forge recall before a new hypothesis, use seed 0 and the public initializer, keep task conditions fixed, declare the recipe delta and budget, and stop on the frozen rules. Exit condition: an accepted compatible calibration report and a frozen independent confirmation question, rather than another best-observed count.
4. **Pursue one bounded mechanism question.** Use saved-state diagnostics to choose between the unresolved acquisition/precision, settling or recovery failure, instead of combining several repairs. Hold out the confirmation tasks from selection. Report terminal margins, all required regressions and actual compute. No experiment is registered or authorized by this audit.
5. **Demonstrate a useful user workload.** After the measurement repairs, prioritize one realistic dataset/workflow with a credible matched baseline, fixed training budget, fidelity and coverage metrics, memory/throughput, and a clean reproduction command. For a research paper, the burden is causal and external validity; for a library release, it is reliable behavior, compatibility and a scoped useful example. Either would be more valuable than expanding the toy count alone.

Keep the current BISPF research default unless a replacement meets the declared adoption process. Atlas's higher observed full-suite pass count and BISPF's stronger counted-view projection answer different, currently confounded questions; neither establishes a universal winner.
