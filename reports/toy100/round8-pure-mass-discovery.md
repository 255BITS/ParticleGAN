# Held-out mass test for later support discovery

A pure data filter recovered **eligibility** for the omitted eighth support region after a false seven-group bootstrap. It did not train a GAN or add a native learner update. The same filter cannot recover a component that lies inside an existing fixed identity ball; that geometry remains a concrete blocker to a general fixed-support controller.

The proposal used only one previously rejected native D bank, at absolute update 2403, after two conditioned banks had confirmed seven groups. Its SHA-256 `3cd9aeb2459a77d6719afe3a89dbfd3d16a95af3df2b89180e45f855093fd5e3` matches the archived neural false-confirmation fixture; all 22 samples from omitted mode 0 were rejected by the old fixed balls. The largest deterministic rejected MST group supplied 21 samples, center `(2.995862, 0.010982)`, and fixed radius `0.154630`. Its distance clearance from the old balls was `1.163875`, so the candidate ball is disjoint from all seven old balls. The real mode label was used only to audit the fixture, never to select this region.

For a frozen proposed ball \(A_j\), only later independent real banks are counted. If \(n_b\) is the total number of real rows after bank \(b\), including rows in old regions, and \(\hat p_b\) is its hit fraction, the one-sided lower bound is

\[L_{j,b}=\hat p_b-\sqrt{\frac{\log\{b(b+1)/\alpha_j\}}{2n_b}},\qquad \alpha_j=\frac{\delta}{j(j+1)},\quad \delta=0.01.\]

The rule admits only when \(L_{j,b}>1/N\), where the actual generator has \(N=12\) equally weighted particles. Hoeffding's bound assigns at most \(\alpha_j/[b(b+1)]\) error to that checkpoint; summing over all future bank checkpoints and uniquely indexed, adaptively proposed candidates is at most \(\delta\), conditional on stationary IID validation rows and proposal geometry frozen before its own validation draws. The implementation checks once per full bank, rejects reused proposal and consecutive duplicate-bank bytes, and uses no target labels or evaluation quality as a selection signal. A later native implementation would need to persist candidate IDs, frozen geometry, counts, and bank chronology across exact resumes; this pure filter does not supply that controller.

With the predeclared 96-bank limit, the first candidate crossed the threshold at validation bank **70**, after 8,960 new real samples: 998 hits, empirical mass `0.111384`, lower bound `0.083624`, threshold `0.083333`. A conditioned single old-mode tail yielded only one rejected point and no proposal. The output-only diagnostic first placed 12 points against the old seven centers (7 modes/HQ 1.0); one quadratic MM step after appending the admitted eighth center still gave 7 modes/HQ `0.909912`, while a second gave 8 modes/HQ 1.0. The second MM score was added after the original v1 one-step diagnostic had been observed; **the region, bound, data stream, and bank-70 admission are identical in v1 and v2**. Neither MM step is a neural update or a promotion gate.

The geometric falsifier fixes only the old ring centers 0 and 4. Their half-separation ball radius is 3, while the adjacent true center 1 is inside an old ball. All 128 conditioned mode-1 samples were absorbed; **zero were rejected**. The zero-noise component at center 1 is entirely invisible to a rejected-only rule regardless of its mass. With Gaussian noise, extremely rare tails can leave the ball, but their rejected mass need not exceed `1/12` or identify a new component. This case is a scope test, not evidence of a target shift in the user's stationary training task. More generally, a high-mass disjoint ball can contain diffuse tails rather than a distinct component, and a true component with mass at most `1/12` is deliberately ineligible.
