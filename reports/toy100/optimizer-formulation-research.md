# Research-backed optimizer and formulation comparisons

The [winning production recipe](shared22/README.md) now passes **22/22** with ordinary Adam after changing κ to 1.176. Most tuning in the retained accuracy and shared-recipe ledgers has used Adam, relativistic pairing, and the same capped gradient penalty. The following comparisons change the optimization method or regularizer directly. They are hypotheses to test under the frozen gates, not claimed solutions.

| Method | Relevant mechanism | Experimental adaptation | Gradient-evaluation cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Optimistic Adam | Correct the current preconditioned direction using the previous direction, targeting cycling in adversarial training | Apply uniformly to G, D, and learned prior; preserve the existing alternating loop; compare full optimism and explicitly experimental damping | One gradient evaluation per existing player update |
| ExtraAdam | Evaluate a look-ahead point before committing the update from the original point | Consider after the lower-cost optimistic comparison; document the simultaneous/alternating convention and moment updates | Standard extrapolation needs an additional gradient evaluation; past-extrapolation is a distinct alternative |
| RpGAN + R1 + R2 | Zero-centered discriminator penalties on both real and generated samples | Use the repository's existing `a_r1r2` arm with shared coefficients, retaining frozen host architectures | Same gradient-norm computation pattern as the current capped penalty |

Optimistic Adam follows Algorithm 1 of [Training GANs with Optimism](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00141). With bias-corrected Adam direction `u_t`, the scratch update is `θ -= lr_t * ((1 + α) * u_t - α * u_(t-1))`; the previous direction starts at zero. Both terms use the current scheduled learning rate. `α=1` gives the paper's direction, while `.25` and `.5` are declared damped experiments. Tests must cover changing rates, saved-state replay, zero-optimism parity, and a bilinear cycling example. The paper's convergence result for particular games is not a guarantee for these nonlinear, alternating toy trainers.

[A Variational Inequality Perspective on Generative Adversarial Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10551) distinguishes standard extrapolation, extrapolation from the past, and their moment updates. Any later comparison must report gradient calls and wall time as well as training updates; doubling gradient work cannot be presented as a free convergence improvement.

[R3GAN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05441) motivates combining relativistic pairing with zero-centered R1 and R2 regularization. This repository already implements relativistic pairing and the joint penalty. The proposed comparison changes the regularizer and its global coefficient; it does not reproduce R3GAN's architecture or claim that its local convergence assumptions hold here.

Candidate configurations, source, and stage order are fixed before each screen. Cheap older-toy rejection precedes a fresh full-19 replay and then the native/common-22 gate. Skipped hosts are untested, and scratch optimizer changes cannot qualify for the production common gate until their policy, application receipts, checkpoint state, and source identity are integrated and replayed. The existing 100-mode limits, original host budgets, seeds, and live-weight requirements remain fixed.

The first [eight R1+R2 candidates](r1r2-transfer.md) all failed the ordered cheap screen, so none advanced to a full-19 replay or native training. The [measured screening-cost audit](fail-fast-stage-order.md) documents the stage order, conditional rejection rates, and its limits. The [six-row Optimistic Adam screen](optimistic-adam-scratch.md) also found no qualifying candidate: all shared-core rows failed trajectory; noiseless-core rows advanced farther but failed stripes or mode-hold. All 13 attempted episodes passed strict source/optimizer receipt validation, while the production gate correctly rejected the scratch policy. No row advanced to a full-19 or native replay. These finite comparisons did not improve on the 22/22 ordinary-Adam winner; they do not establish that the research methods cannot work with further tuning.
