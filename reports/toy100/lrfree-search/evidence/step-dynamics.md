# Adam step dynamics: does AMSGrad fix the LR cycling? (PR202 mechanism test)

**Question:** "Did AMSGrad fix the cycling you saw earlier in the LR?" There are two separate phenomena, and the answer differs for each.

| phenomenon | what it looks like | caused by Adam second-moment decay ("creep")? | does AMSGrad fix it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Short-horizon cycling** (mode_hold 1200, images 600; the constant-LR wave-1 finding) | HQ swings 1.0 -> 0.0-0.6 -> 1.0 within a few hundred updates during and after acquisition | **No.** It still cycles when the denominator is flat. At lr .001 the D `sqrt(v_hat)` changes by <=10% over updates 400-1200 in both arms, yet each arm has 2-3 HQ drops of >=.3. | **No.** All 4 PR202 mode_hold pairs FAIL in both arms. Over the 7 complete mode_hold pairs: AMSGrad 2 PASS / 15 checks, plain 3 PASS / 41 checks. |
| **B. Long-horizon collapse** (stationary 7500, ring_shift after a long hold) | a clean hold (HQ~.98) collapses to HQ 0 in <=10-20 updates, then re-acquires | **Yes**, but the thing that creeps is the Adam *gain* `lr/sqrt(v_hat)` (about x2 in the first ~800-update hold; by 7000-7400, D x7-8 and G x3-4), not the typical realized step. | **Yes.** Stationary: 0 vs 9 departures. Both switch interventions confirm the cause (below). |

In short, AMSGrad fixes the slow collapse after a long hold and does not fix the fast cycling. The fast cycling is an intrinsic D/G oscillation at these LRs and batch sizes.

## Method

`analysis/instrument.py` imports `harness/screen.py` unchanged. It overrides `Context.rate_row`, which runs after every `trainer.step`. Every 10 updates it writes one row per optimizer group (G, prior, D) to `instr.jsonl`. Each row holds the median and p90 over elements of `|lr*m_hat/(sqrt(v_hat)+eps)|` (using `max_exp_avg_sq` under AMSGrad), `||dtheta||` from a parameter snapshot taken after the previous update, grad RMS (`m_hat`; beta1=0, so this is the raw post-guard gradient), median `sqrt(v_hat)` (the value used and the plain one), and the fraction of elements where the AMSGrad max is binding. All reads are CPU copies. There are no CUDA ops on learner tensors and no RNG use.

- **Non-perturbation check:** `analysis/verify_identical.py`. All 8 instrumented runs are **bitwise identical** to their pool runs: every metrics row except wall-clock (24/24, 460/460 and 750/750 rows) and every rates row (1200, 4600 and 7500 per-update LR rows).
- **Consistency check:** the predicted Adam update norm equals the measured `||dtheta||` for G and D (e.g. 1.07733349 vs 1.07733350).
- **Units:** "step" is always shown divided by the group LR (x lr). Windows are the median of the 10-update rows within +-100 updates.
- **Runs:** configs come from `runs/<cand>/{overrides,options}.json`, with outputs in `analysis/instr/<cand>/<task>/`. Tables are regenerated by `analysis/step_tables.py`.

Reading the tables:

- **Gain creep under plain Adam.** The gain `lr/sqrt(v_hat)` grows by about 2x in the roughly 800-update first hold (D and G both 1/0.55). This matches PR202's "about 2x over about 1000 steps".
- **The typical realized step barely creeps** (D median 1.2x in the first hold, 1.06x over the whole run). `v` tracks the shrinking gradients, so plain Adam keeps the quiet-time step at a constant fraction of lr instead of letting it shrink.
- **The realized step is not what separates the arms.** In the first hold the AMSGrad step grew *more* (1.5x) and AMSGrad did not collapse.
- **What separates them is the gain:** by 1450, plain Adam amplifies any gradient fluctuation 1.4-1.6x more than AMSGrad does, and 2-6x more later in the run.
- **Under AMSGrad** the step shrinks with the gradient: 0.37-0.64x over the run.

- **No ramp in the realized step before a departure.** Across the 6 departures, the [s-100, s-20] window is within 0.6-1.4x of baseline, and 1.05-1.4x at 1480 and 3910, the two departures that go to HQ ~0.
- **The step explodes in the same 10-update row as the HQ drop,** with G leading by at most 10 updates (G p90 at s-10 is 5x base at 1480 and 2.6x at 5790). The collapse is a fast instability, not a slow threshold crossing of the step.
- **The slow variable is the gain.** At the collapse, D `sqrt(v_hat)` is 0.15-0.48x its level at arrival, so the gain is 2-7x higher.

**Causal test: switch interventions.** These use `instrument.py --switch-amsgrad STEP:on|off`. After update STEP, every group is switched to AMSGrad (the max is seeded with the current `v`) or back to plain Adam. Each run is bitwise identical to its pool run up to the switch: 100/100 and 300/300 checks.

| run | departures after 1000 | checks passing after 1000 | min HQ after 1000 | D `sqrt(v_hat)` 2990 / 3010 / 7000 | D step p90 x lr at 3000 / 3010 / ~7000 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| plain (pool config) | 8 (1480, 1970, 4370, ...) | 529/650 | 0.00 | 5.0e-4 / 5.0e-4 / 2.0e-4 | .14 / .25 / .25 |
| **plain -> AMSGrad after 1000** | **0** | **650/650** | 0.98 | 7.3e-4 / 7.3e-4 / 7.2e-4 | .12 / .15 / .09 |
| AMSGrad (pool config) | 0 | 650/650 | 0.98 | 9.7e-4 / 9.7e-4 / 9.4e-4 | .12 / .15 / .09 |
| **AMSGrad -> plain after 3000** | **14 (3010, 3500, 3570, ...)** | 526/650 | 0.00 | 9.7e-4 / **3.6e-4** / 1.8e-4 | .12 / **12.8** / .31 |

- **Stopping the denominator decay after 1000** (AMSGrad seeded with the update-1000 `v`) removes every later collapse, including the one plain Adam had at 1480.
- **Releasing 2000 updates of accumulated decay at once** after a clean 2450-update hold: at update 3000, AMSGrad's `sqrt(v_max)/sqrt(v)` was 3.4x for D, 3.6x for G and 3.7x for the prior. Releasing it collapses the equilibrium within 10 updates (HQ .99 -> .00; G grad 8.5e-5 -> 5.3e-2). The run then behaves like plain Adam, with 14 departures.

The trajectories are chaotic after the switch, so these are single deterministic runs, not seed statistics. Still, in both directions, only the denominator was changed from an identical state.

## Conclusions

1. **PR202's mechanism is confirmed for phenomenon B, with one correction.** Under plain Adam with beta2 = .999, `v` decays during a clean hold, so the gain `lr/sqrt(v_hat)` grows: about 2x over the first ~800-update hold and 5-8x by 7000. The typical realized step does *not* creep much, because `v` tracks the shrinking gradients; its p90 grows only about 1.4x. The exception is the sparse prior rows, whose realized step does grow about 2.5-3x by 7000-7400. The failure is abrupt, with no step ramp in the 100 updates before it: a small G/D gradient fluctuation escalates, within 10-20 updates, into a 26-227x G-gradient burst in the deep collapses, because the gain is high. AMSGrad caps the gain at the historical-peak `v` (less the bias-correction factor), so the step shrinks with the gradient and fluctuations stay at or below 3x.
2. **The switch interventions show the cause directly.** Capping the denominator at its update-1000 value removes all 8 later collapses. Releasing about 3.5x of pent-up decay at 3000 collapses a clean equilibrium within 10 updates.
3. **Phenomenon A (the mode_hold/image cycling from the constant-LR sweep) is not creep, and AMSGrad does not fix it.** It happens with flat denominators and statistically flat step sizes. It is an intrinsic D/G oscillation of the small-batch game at these constant LRs. The levers for A are elsewhere: LR/step level, the critic/penalty configuration (the DV12-family pairs pass mode_hold in both arms) or averaging, not the second-moment estimator.
4. **Recommendation.** Keep AMSGrad (or any non-decaying denominator) for constant-LR, run-forever configs: it is required for B and costs little on short tasks. Do not count it as a fix for the short-horizon cycling; screen A separately.
5. **Note for the PR text.** Torch AMSGrad still raises the gain by `1/sqrt(bc2(t_peak))` during the first ~3000 updates, so it is not a flat gain early in training.
