# Independent review of the one-step virtual-critic derivative

The one-step unroll source `pr84_one_step_unroll.py` (SHA-256 `33fa46e99572d99df02d8c291bf1329e5c668bb92af718dc5482d2a80ced0d78`) defines a coherent local scalar surrogate. Its inner sharp D loss is evaluated on the actual fixed native D batch; the graph-preserving `b_cap` retains fake-to-D dependence while reproducing the native D loss value and D gradient exactly. The virtual D point uses a detached saved post-Adam diagonal metric and no extra moment update. The detached and full-chain controls have identical virtual D parameter values and use the same actual G batch and fixed 0.15 spatial stencil. Inspection found no missing graph path or unexpected persistent D, noise, or training-state write.

The original two finite-difference checks use float32 modules and h ≈ .0025/.0013 at step 472 and .0030/.0015 at step 1325. They miss the analytic derivative by 4–19%. Astra's separate `pr84_one_step_unroll_audit.py` (source SHA-256 `9dab2a5d69cca7e051b30c67f7783e54c95755b11566746ff7c15153c612b7f0`, result SHA-256 `1bb2e3b2764190e3dcb457e84a5a28d67ac5266a1ca6ac96558f8b5afbaf50f9`) reproduces roughly 9% error at the same coarse h **even in float64**. Both endpoints cross hundreds of LeakyReLU masks; the step-472 case also crosses active penalty-cap masks. Thus the coarse discrepancy is not evidence of a missing derivative or a float32-only error.

At predeclared h/2¹², h/2¹⁴, and h/2¹⁶ in a float64 copy, both saved states have zero generator, starting-D, virtual-D, and cap-mask switches at both endpoints. Central differences then agree with the analytic derivative to relative error ≤ `1.08e-10` at 472 and ≤ `1.20e-9` at 1325. The double-copy gradient has cosine greater than `.999999999999` with the original native gradient. This validates the local scalar derivative at these two states. It does not validate the proposed update as a stable game method, the native float32 finite-difference limit, or the direction of its actual bounded G proposal. The unrolled full-chain field still points outward in the failing state, so these derivative checks alone do not establish a repair.
