# What cumulative rejected mass can establish

The two-bank learner can confirm an incomplete support set. In the [native three-update counterexample](continuous-evidence/round7-two-bank-false-confirm/manifest.json), the next full-target D bank gives 22 real observations from the missing ring component, but a frozen-cell learner rejects all 22. Accumulating rejected data could eventually support discovery of that separated component. The statistical claim needs a region fixed before its validation samples arrive.

Let a candidate region \(A_j\) be any measurable set chosen from all data through its proposal bank, and then held fixed. On subsequent independent samples from a stationary target, let \(X_{j,t}=1\{R_t\in A_j\}\), \(S_{j,n}=\sum_{t=1}^{n}X_{j,t}\), and \(p_j=P(R\in A_j)\). Count *all* future real samples in the denominator \(n\); do not divide only by rejected observations. If candidates are numbered in proposal order, allocate \(\alpha_j=\alpha/[j(j+1)]\). The one-sided lower bound

\[
L_{j,n}=\frac{S_{j,n}}{n}-\sqrt{\frac{\log(n(n+1)/\alpha_j)}{2n}}
\]

obeys \(P(\exists j,n: L_{j,n}>p_j)\le\alpha\). For each fixed candidate this follows from conditional Hoeffding and \(\sum_{n\ge1}1/[n(n+1)]=1\); summing \(\alpha_j\) gives the familywise bound even when each *future* candidate is chosen adaptively from earlier banks. A declaration could admit a region once \(L_{j,n}>1/N\), where \(N\) is the number of equal-weight generated particles. Under the same stationary IID premise, a region with true mass \(p_j>1/N\) eventually crosses. For the ring's \(N=12\), a region of exact mass \(1/8\), \(\alpha=.01\), and the first allocation \(\alpha_1=.005\), the deterministic bound evaluated at \(S/n=1/8\) crosses after 6,591 independent validation samples, or 52 full banks of 128. Actual crossing time depends on the sample path.

This theorem controls false *mass admission* for regions with \(p_j\le1/N\). It does not show that a region is one semantic mode, that its empirical center is a good target, or that a neural G/prior fit can realize it. A diffuse tail with mass above \(1/N\) legitimately passes. A component with mass at or below \(1/N\) cannot be guaranteed to pass this strict rule, even if a coverage metric values it. A changed boundary or reuse of the proposal bank for validation invalidates the bound; a changed region must receive a new candidate index and future validation stream. This is a stationary-target conditional result, not a target-shift guarantee.

There is a decisive geometric limit to **outside-cell-only** discovery. With frozen reference centers at 0 and 4, the half-separation acceptance radius is 2. A newly observed true component at 1 lies inside the 0 cell. Even if it has arbitrarily large target mass, its rejected mass is exactly zero in a zero-noise version of the model. No amount of rejected-data evidence can discover it. The first two moments maintained by the current learner may reveal drift or excess within-cell spread, but they cannot alone locate and certify a separate subcomponent without additional distributional assumptions or retained sample geometry. The fixed-ball confidence rule should therefore be tested only under the explicit premise that every missing component has a separated measurable region outside established cells; it does not repair the general acquisition problem.
