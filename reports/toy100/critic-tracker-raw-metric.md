# A critic tracker whose noise does not cancel its own Adam denominator

The update-1325 diagnosis finds D component variance `.00728322` against squared mean metric norm `.00231648`. Averaging sixteen fixed banks removes most of that variance at one point, but the subsequent contiguous saved-state check still fails 24 of 44 quality checks. Noise is one problem; the measured G-only fixed-bank direction is anti-monotone (Rayleigh `−.137772`) and its local D-then-G map expands that direction by `1.086325`. A variance-reduced critic alone is therefore **not** a candidate that qualifies this game.

One cheap, falsifiable critic-estimator rule is the same-bank recursive field

`v_n = g(x_n;ξ_n) + (1−1/n)[v_(n−1)−g(x_(n−1);ξ_n)]`,

where `x=(D,G,prior)`, `ξ_n` contains the *same* native real examples, prior indices and noise for both evaluations, and `n` counts distinct observed D banks. Initialize `v_1=g(x_1;ξ_1)`. Use `v_n` as D's first moment, but update Adam's β₂=`.999` **second** moment from the ordinary uncorrected `g(x_n;ξ_n)²`; keep nominal rate `.00425`, ε=`1e−8`, and one D moment advance per outer update. The `1/n` is an estimator's statistical averaging weight, not a decay of the parameter-update rate. The same-bank difference supplies an immediate response when the model changes. It needs one extra D gradient evaluation on the new bank at the previous joint state, without an extra data draw. A faithful native adapter would replay the relevant noise and `b_cap` at both states, and carry `v`, prior joint state, raw second moment, and bank count in checkpoints. Before the noise horizon ends, the previous evaluation must also account for the previous noise policy; applying this fixed-law formula across a changing noise clock would be incorrect.

At a fixed true D root with independent banks and finite variance, the tracker is an online sample mean. More generally, writing `e_n=v_n−F(x_n)`, fresh-bank conditional means give `E[e_n | history]=(1−1/n)e_(n−1)`. If stochastic fields have mean-square Lipschitz constant `L`, the same-bank difference noise scales with `L²||x_n−x_(n−1)||²/B`. Multiplying the error recursion by `n` shows a bound proportional to `n⁻² sum_(s≤n) s² E||x_s−x_(s−1)||²/B + O(1/(Bn))`. Thus variance tends to zero in a sufficiently settling region; **a persistent or merely `1/√n`-scale moving joint state need not satisfy this condition**. The frozen target law alone is not enough.

The raw second moment is essential. At the fixed scalar root `F(x)=x`, with `g(x,ξ)=x+ξ` and symmetric independent `ξ∈{−1,+1}`, `v_n=x+mean(ξ_1,…,ξ_n)` has variance `1/n`. The raw squared gradient has expectation `1` at `x=0`; an Adam denominator built from `v_n²` instead falls with the estimate. Before ε dominates, normalizing by that shrinking denominator can cancel the variance reduction and leave a step of order the nominal rate. The [no-RNG scalar check](critic_tracker_raw_metric_toy.py) uses a declared `++--` illustrative sequence, not an IID training run. At bank 9,999, it yields a raw-metric step `4.25e−7` versus a tracked-metric step `.00117049`. A model error of `+.35` after bank 10,000 changes the tracked field by the full `.35` on the next bank; the raw-metric step is `.00148731`, while a tracked-squared-metric step would be `.134322`. D's own-curvature cap `3` is inactive in this scalar shock (`ρ=.00425` or `.38367`), so the cap alone does not remove the discrepancy. Fixed ε eventually dominates even the tracked denominator, but only after the estimator scale falls near `1e−8`; the calculation concerns realistic finite horizons, not a claim about the mathematical limit `n→∞` with fixed ε.

In the neural host, positive expected raw-gradient variance is not a pathwise lower bound on an EMA denominator. Dead or nearly deterministic coordinates and rare quiet streaks can still have a small denominator. Also, using the **current** raw bank for that denominator correlates the preconditioner with the tracked numerator: even when `v_n` is an unbiased field estimate before normalization, `v_n/(sqrt(s_n)+ε)` need not be an unbiased preconditioned field. A lagged raw metric would be predictable from past banks but would introduce its own response lag. The raw-metric construction prevents the *automatic* cancellation shown by the scalar toy; it does not bound every coordinate or prove perpetual rest. This proposal concerns D only. At a constant-critic GAN state, each G sample gradient can vanish and G's raw second moment can decay toward ε; G's own-field guard or a separate genuine metric bound remains necessary after a model perturbation.

The host's bounds do not supply a general deterministic stability theorem. For a scalar convex own-field with unbounded normalized curvature `h`, the bounded update multiplier is `1−min(h,κ)`: at `h=3`, D's `κ=3` gives `−2` (expansion), while G's `κ=.25` gives `.75`. In a pure bilinear D-then-G game both own curvatures are zero, so neither bound acts; the alternating map has determinant one and cannot be strictly contractive. These are illustrative games, not claims that the frozen neural host is bilinear or scalar convex.

The smallest host-specific falsifier is a **saved-state, no-training** D-field test: at a passing late state, evaluate the raw and transported D gradients on the exact next native bank and one fixed joint perturbation using identical samples/noise. Compare their held-out means, paired-difference variance, and the actual D proposal under raw versus tracked second moments; also check whether D's own-curvature cap changes branch. Then measure the fixed-bank D→G local map with that proposal. If the tracked D direction is biased/stale, the raw metric is ill-conditioned, or the mean map remains quality-damaging, stop before any warm continuation. No HQ or known target centers enter the estimator.

[Cutkosky and Orabona's STORM paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/b8002139cdde66b87638f7f91d169d96-Abstract.html) supplies the recursive same-sample mechanism for nonconvex stochastic **minimization**; its rate is not a theorem for this Adam-driven game. [Chavdarova et al.](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/hash/58a2fc6ed39fd083f55d4182bf88826d-Abstract.html) show that stochastic noise can prevent convergence of a game method whose batch version converges and study variance-reduced extragradient under their game assumptions. The [2026 VR-SDA-A preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.23034v1) combines a STORM field with same-batch curvature checks, but its stated proof assumes a Lipschitz Jacobian, bounded iterates, bounded-variance oracle and local variational stability `μ>0`; none has been established for this LeakyReLU/`b_cap` neural host. The proposal here is a diagnostic hypothesis, not a transfer of those guarantees.
