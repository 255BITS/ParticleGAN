# A cheap analytic bound does not certify the blurred-target likelihood update

This note analyzes the frozen **pure-output** cold cumulative update 14, not a neural training run. Let `p_h` be the observed-real empirical measure convolved with `N(0,h²I)`, and `q_v(Y)` the equal-weight 12-Gaussian model density with `v=h²+σ²`. The continuous target is `J(Y)=E_{X~p_h}[−log q_v(X;Y)]`. Standard exact EM monotonicity applies when the E-step uses the true posterior responsibilities **at each X** and integrates them exactly; its center update needs `E_p[r_j(X)X]/E_p[r_j(X)]`. These logistic-normal moments are not replaced by exact closed forms merely because the Gaussian components have fixed variance. The frozen GH5 rule runs EM on its discrete quadrature target and therefore guarantees descent of GH5, not of continuous `J` or GH9. This distinction agrees with the original [EM monotonicity argument](https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1977.tb01600.x) and work on [Gaussian-mixture KL approximation](https://dihana.cps.unizar.es/proceedings/ICASSP/2007/pdfs/0400317.pdf); neither source certifies this host's finite GH rule.

There is a closed-form sandwich for `J`. Convexity of `−log` gives the lower bound `L(Y)=−R⁻¹ Σ_i log q_{v+h²}(r_i;Y)`. A variational log-sum bound using one responsibility vector per real center, independent of its Gaussian perturbation, gives the upper bound `U(Y)=−R⁻¹ Σ_i log q_v(r_i;Y)+d h²/(2v)`, here `d=2`. The optimal constant responsibility vector is the mixture posterior at `r_i`; the formula also follows from `∇²_x(−log q_v)≼I/v`. Thus `U(Y_new)<L(Y_old)` would certify a true continuous-`J` decrease without quadrature. The inequalities hold in exact arithmetic; the values below are float64 diagnostics, not directed-rounding computer proofs.

`U(after)−L(before)=+2.488696641`, whereas the GH9 whole-update decrease is only `0.001890470`. The analytic sandwich is far too loose to certify this useful-looking local step. More careful cellwise Gaussian integration could tighten it, but comparing GH5 and GH9 is an error estimate, not a deterministic enclosure; a rigorous interval method needs Gaussian tail and floating-point error bounds and has no established cheap cost on this 2-D, growing-history workload. [Guaranteed mixture bounds for univariate models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.05850) do not directly establish a practical 2-D certificate here.

The exact finite empirical-center NLL `−R⁻¹Σ_i log q_v(r_i;Y)` is much cheaper than target convolution and needs no quadrature. It is **a different objective**: at cold `σ=0` it needs a declared positive kernel width, and finite real samples can omit a true mode or reward an outlier. Decreasing that empirical objective does not prove density fidelity, mode coverage, or uninterrupted stability. The selected next diagnostic uses GH9 as a *declared finite objective* for final acceptance and treats higher-order quadrature as numerical audit; no continuous-likelihood claim follows from it.
