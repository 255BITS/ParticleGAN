The selected BISPF run loses the rare component after temporarily populating it. It also retains large full-component covariance errors in two common components. The rare component is absent at every scheduled check from update 400 through 1,200. This is a coverage-and-shape failure, and the original sustained FAIL remains unchanged.

At updates 200, 250, 300 and 350, the rare component has 175, 263, 216 and 216 nearest-centre assignments out of 4,096; the endpoint counts are **2,488 / 1,203 / 405 / 0**, against target masses **.55 / .30 / .13 / .02**. No scheduled observation passes all gates. Mass TV passes 23/24 checks and SW passes 19/24, showing why these aggregate distances cannot replace the explicit rare gate. Full-component covariance fails 24/24.

The scorer assigns by nearest target centre, computes covariance around the assigned sample mean, and returns shape error 1 and eigen ratio 0 for fewer than ten assigned samples. Thus the rare eigen ratio of zero is an **empty-component sentinel**, not evidence of a measured flattened ellipse. Common-component full covariance errors are **3.04735 / 2.34933 / .67387**, while their four-sigma-core errors are **.25514 / .41184 / .67387**. The first two components therefore have large contributions outside the scored core; their exact outlier geometry requires the original sample tensors. Removing those tails would still leave zero rare occupancy.

The current problem-list prose describes an earlier BISPF run with retained rare mass and eigen ratio .004. That explanation does not describe this selected measurement. The older [BISPF-v1 result](../../bispf/README.md) remains separate: its minimum mass ratio .45166 cannot be substituted for this run's zero.

The 2% target supplies 2.56 rare examples per batch of 128 in expectation. A true-target IID sample of 4,096 has probability approximately **1.15e-36** of containing no rare draws. This is an illustrative target-law calculation; nearest-centre assignment and learned sampling are separately defined. Repeated zero assigned samples provide evidence of a served coverage defect. They do not identify which update field caused its onset.

The frozen BISPF code uses detached, median-distance-scaled Sinkhorn coupling inside the critic penalty, a gradient cap plus floor on endpoint/chord critic gradients, and latent SBNP regularization. These do not directly enforce a nonzero output population per target component. Uniform prior mixture weights do not enforce uniform or prescribed output-component occupancy: many learned rows may map to the same component. Conversely, five expected prior rows for a 2% component do not establish an impossibility for the nonzero-width learned MoG.

The loss of rare assignments occurs between scheduled observations 350 and 400. The continuous-flow branch starts at critic call 500, gauge growth at 1,000, and the special harmonic lock at 1,650. Those later branches cannot explain the already observed onset; ordinary residual cooling is present earlier and remains unmeasured here. There are no convolution parameters, so the selected quintic convolution change supplies no causal explanation.
