# Force distortion, round five

Preregistered hypothesis: damped empirical output-kernel force filtering improves native precision while retaining the direction blend's full conditional identity passes. This bounded diagnostic has a 21,600 worker-second ceiling, retains original gates and does not confer ordinary qualification. Results are pending; no adoption or global repair is claimed.

The matched comparison will cover full 7k grid100, trajectory, residual student, mid-scale identity and Gaussian smoke/own stability. Their two-arm full reservation is 19,440 seconds. Rare/broad full reservations do not fit; they remain outside this declared scope. Saved-state probes and software checks have a separate 2,160-second ceiling.

The [saved force traces](saved-force-traces.json) precede mechanism selection. Native uses the original winner's frozen source and one discarded actual next D/G step, restoring its complete context and named streams. Conditional probes fix the saved critic and retain the original objectives; the unequal-width vector probe uses separately labelled deterministic kernel cubature. These are endpoint cohorts, not a causal decomposition of training history. Network and prior linear contributions add with relative error below 1.3e-7. Native raw shared-gradient motion sends 5.08% of rows uphill against their own output force, versus 3.61% after DualNorm; the vector figures are 30.18% and 21.19%. Finite motion is close to its linear approximation in these probes, with relative errors 2.49% native and 2.21% vector. Neither aggregate loss descent nor a useful average direction protects each row. Conditional per-loss normalized proposals have nonadditivity ratios .996914 and 1.215825, reproducing the original endpoint finding.

The sole candidate filters the unchanged total objective's sample-output force f through u = lambda (J J^T + lambda I)^-1 f, approximated by exactly four matrix-free conjugate-gradient iterations (or earlier numerical convergence). J includes the joint generator and learned prior; lambda = ||J^T f||^2 / ||f||^2 is one global force-Rayleigh damping law, with zero-force identity handling. It replaces only J^T f by J^T u in the parameter gradient. Direct latent regularizers remain intact. The existing full DualNorm proposal, rates, critic optimizer and common-descent direction blend then execute unchanged. Thus this is one global preconditioning intervention, not exact natural gradient, a Fisher estimate, a finite acceptance controller or a changed loss. No evaluator geometry, target labels, component sigma or extra supervision enters the rule.

[Jacot, Gabriel and Hongler's NTK paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2018/file/5a4be1fa34e62bb8a6ec6b91d2462f5a-Paper.pdf) motivates tracing J J^T coupling in function space. Its infinite-width gradient-flow convergence results do not apply as a guarantee to this finite adversarial trainer. [Amari's natural-gradient paper](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976698300017746) motivates distinguishing parameter geometry from function/distribution geometry; the empirical output metric here is an analogy rather than its Fisher construction. [Martens and Sutskever's Hessian-free account](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~jmartens/docs/HF_book_chapter.pdf) supplies the primary matrix-free CG/preconditioning context. Our finite four-iteration filter and its ridge are separately testable implementation choices, not a theorem of density convergence.
